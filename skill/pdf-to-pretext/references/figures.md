# Figures and tables

A figure is cropped from the page of the original, split into panels where it is a
composite, and described in words written from the picture itself.  Recreating a drawing
as a PreFigure diagram is a further step, worth taking only when asked.

## How the figure is stored decides how it is taken

`pdfimages -list transcription/original.pdf` lists the raster pictures embedded in the
PDF, with the page and the natural size of each.  A figure that is not listed is a vector
drawing.  Either way the labels are usually typeset over the picture by LaTeX, so pulling
the embedded picture out loses every label.  Crop the region of the page instead: that
keeps picture and labels together, raster and vector alike.

## Cropping

`scripts/crop-figures.py transcription/original.pdf transcription/figure-spec.json assets`
crops every panel of every figure.  The specification is a list, in page order:

```json
{"figure": "5.4", "page": 10, "panels": ["figure-rectangle-general", "figure-rectangle-degenerate"],
 "split": ["cols", 2]}
```

with `"split"` one of `null` (one panel), `["cols", n]` (n panels across), or
`["grid", [n1, n2, ...]]` (rows of n1, n2, ... panels).  It finds each figure from the
positions of the text around it and the ink between, and writes every panel as SVG and as
PDF, so that `image/@source` needs no extension.  Read the script's own header for the
rules it applies and for the options that fit another page layout.

- **Look at the contact sheets** it writes before authoring.  Every wrong box so far was
  found on a contact sheet and none any other way.
- **Page numbers.**  The specification wants PDF page numbers, which can differ from the
  printed ones.
- **By hand**, one crop is `pdftocairo -svg -f P -l P -x X -y Y -W W -H H -paperw W -paperh H`
  (and again with `-pdf`), in points.  Without `-paperw` and `-paperh` the output keeps
  the whole page with the crop in place.

## Composite figures

- Several embedded pictures, each with its own lettered subcaption, are subfigures: an
  outer `figure` holding a `sidebyside` of `figure` panels, the lettering kept.
- One picture holding several drawings, with no lettering, is cut into `image` panels in
  a `sidebyside`.  Letter panels only where the original letters them.
- A `sidebyside` needs two panels or more.  A two-part figure stacked vertically under
  one caption therefore stays one image.  A grid of two, two, and one becomes three and
  two in an `sbsgroup`, which itself needs two `sidebyside`s.
- **Panel widths come from the pictures' natural sizes.**  Three drawings 82, 179, and
  100 points wide are `widths="21% 46% 26%"`; equal widths would rescale them against
  each other.
- An outer `figure` needs a `caption`, which may be empty when the original has only
  subcaptions.

## Describing

Every `image` gets a `description`, or a `shortdescription` of at most 125 characters,
written from the picture: what is drawn, what is labeled, what a reader is meant to see.
The caption is the author's; the description is yours, and it is what a reader who cannot
see the picture receives.

## Numbering

PreTeXt numbers figures, tables, and listings on one shared counter.
`figures/@distinct="yes"` in the publication file separates that counter from the
theorem-like blocks.  A paper that numbers its tables separately from its figures will
not agree with the build; record the mismatch (`references/numbering.md`).

## Tables

A table is a `table` with a `title`, holding a `tabular` of `row`s and `cell`s; `col`
carries widths and alignment.  Transcribe the cells from the text layer and check every
row against the page image.

## Checking

Text inside a figure extracts differently from the original and from the build, so
`scripts/compare.py` reports caption and label words as absent.  Read each such run
against the page.

## Recreating a drawing in PreFigure

Optional.  A PreFigure diagram can be drawn as tactile graphics and described part by
part, which a crop cannot.

- `scripts/trace-curve.py` traces one simple curve of one color from a 600-dpi render of
  the crop, giving points for a PreFigure `spline` with chord-length `t-values`.
  Crossings with gaps are one spline drawn piecewise by `domain`, with the crossing points
  as knots.
- `scripts/trace-figure.py` vectorizes a whole colored line drawing (curves by color,
  arrowheads, dots, dashes, fills, highlight bands), to be turned into a diagram of
  `polygon`s with labels from the text layer.  Its color ranges were tuned on one paper's
  drawings; expect to tune them again, and read the overlay it writes after every change.
- The diagrams are generated by PreTeXt's script (component `prefigure`, formats `svg`
  and `pdf`) into `generated-assets/` before the HTML and PDF builds.
